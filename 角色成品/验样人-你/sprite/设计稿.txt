A top-down game sprite design sheet for the character in the reference image, drawn large but
built to survive being shrunk to about forty pixels tall. Plain white background: nothing at all is drawn between or under the figures — no ground
line, no shadow, no baseline, no frame. The gap between any two figures is pure empty white
from the top of the image to the bottom.

The same one character drawn five times in a row, evenly spaced, all the same height, their feet all
level with each other, seen from a fixed three-quarter overhead angle so a little of the top of
the head shows: facing the viewer · facing away · facing left · facing right · facing the viewer
again with one arm raised in a greeting.

THE FIFTH FIGURE: identical to the first in every way except the near arm, which bends up at the
elbow so the open hand sits beside the head at eye level. The hand stays level with the eyes and
never rises above the top of the head, and the feet stay level with the others, so all five
figures occupy exactly the same height. The raised hand is out to the side of the head, clear
of the face, never crossing in front of it. The raised forearm reads as one clean block clear of the
body outline, with the hand as one small pale block on top of it.

SPRITE PROPORTION — this is chunkier and squatter than the illustration: three heads tall, and
each figure stands only about one and a half times as tall as it is wide across the shoulders,
so it reads as a stout little block rather than a slim one. The head is one huge rounded mass
taking a third of the total height and is as wide as the shoulders. The body is one short wide
block. The coat ends high enough that both boots stand completely clear below it. The legs are
two tiny stubs, mostly boot, and a gap of background shows between them. The arms are short and
hang close to the body, each hand a single small block.

FACE AT THIS SIZE: a pale block for each eye with one dark dot inside it, and a short mouth
line. That is the entire face.

WHAT SURVIVES: the overall silhouette, the wide storm-cape flap squared across the shoulders, the heavy dark fringe as one solid slab over the eyes, the boots.
Everything small is dropped — the figure carries only large shapes.

VALUE SEPARATION — this is the thing that makes or breaks a sprite: every neighbouring part must
differ clearly in brightness, so the shapes stay apart at forty pixels. Hair, coat, skin and
boots each sit at a visibly different lightness. If the illustration's colour is very dark, the
sprite version of it is lifted to a middle brightness so it separates from the hair.

FLAT BLOCKS: at most six flat colours on the whole figure —
deep-teal #27777F on the folded-open lapels down the chest · lifted slate-blue #4A5568 (the sprite version of the dark navy coat, raised in brightness so it separates from the hair) · bone-white #F1DEA9 · skin #F5E0BC · hair #24211C ·
outline #2E241F. Each area is one single flat colour with hard edges, two or three colours per
garment at the very most.

INK: a warm dark brown #2E241F outline of even weight around the whole figure and around each
colour block. Light from upper left.
